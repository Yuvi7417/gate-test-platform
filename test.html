What is Asymptotic Notation?

Asymptotic Notation is used to describe the growth rate of an algorithm’s time or space complexity as the input size becomes large. It helps compare the efficiency of algorithms independent of hardware.

Types of Asymptotic Notations

  • Big-O Notation (O) – Upper bound of complexity
  • Theta Notation (Θ) – Tight bound of complexity
  • Omega Notation (Ω) – Lower bound of complexity

Big-O, Theta, and Omega Comparison

Notation Meaning Use Case
O(f(n)) Upper bound Worst-case analysis
Θ(f(n)) Tight bound Exact growth rate
Ω(f(n)) Lower bound Best-case analysis

Common Time Complexities

  • O(1) – Constant Time
  • O(log n) – Logarithmic Time
  • O(n) – Linear Time
  • O(n log n) – Linearithmic Time
  • O(n²) – Quadratic Time
  • O(2ⁿ) – Exponential Time

Rules for Asymptotic Analysis

  • Ignore constant factors
  • Consider highest order term
  • Drop lower order terms

Important Concepts in Asymptotic Notation

  • Worst-case complexity
  • Best-case complexity
  • Average-case complexity
  • Order of growth
